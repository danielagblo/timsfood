What we exist to do

Breakfast in many Ghanaian homes is still porridge and koko. The mixes are maize, wheat, rice, millet, sorghum, soy and oats, often enriched, sometimes with ginger or spice. They are for a pot on the stove, not a snack aisle.

Tims Foods puts that pantry online so you can pick a jar, add a family size if the house goes through Weannimix quickly, and have it sent to Accra, Kumasi, Tamale or anywhere else we can reach in Ghana.

We keep the catalogue honest. If a label says from 6 months, that is the instruction we repeat. If it is Hausa Koko powder, it is millet with ginger for a drink or a light porridge — not a dessert mix.

What is in a jar

Each product is packed dry in a plastic jar with a screw lid. Once opened, keep it in a dry cupboard and close the lid after every use so the mix does not cake.

  • Fine porridge mixes cook in a few minutes in boiling water or milk. Stir so they do not lump.
  • Hausa Koko is whisked into water and simmered until it thickens.
  • Oblayo and Ekuegbemi are coarser corn grits. They need a longer simmer than a fine mix.
  • Rice flour and soy powder can go into porridge, drinks or baking, as you already cook them.
